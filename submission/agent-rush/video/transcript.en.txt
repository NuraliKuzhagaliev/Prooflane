An invoice says one price. A contract says another. Which agreement applied, and can a second reviewer reproduce the decision? Prooflane gives accounts payable and procurement teams a review record that connects the price difference to its sources. This edited walkthrough uses fictional data and real browser captures.

The case starts with invoice and contract CSVs. Prooflane matches the supplier, item, and effective date. Seven of eight invoices enter this calculation. One has no active supplied contract and is excluded with a reason. Its billed amount remains visible; exclusion never turns it into estimated savings.

Invoice five hundred and four has two overlapping contract versions. The interface shows the consequence of each eligible price: forty dollars or thirty two dollars. The reviewer explicitly selected C two R. That decision remains attached to the frozen baseline, instead of disappearing into a spreadsheet formula.

This is the saved successful AI run from November ninth, not a new model call during this recording. Vultr Serverless Inference generated Python, and a disposable Docker container executed it. An independent Decimal verifier checked every finding. The result is seven hundred and sixty eight dollars of positive price variance, not recovered cash.

The proof chapter exposes the actual returned model and both Vultr request identifiers. After verification, the model selected the focus of the review guidance. The server inserted the checked amounts. Generated code runs with no network, no host mounts, no injected credentials, and strict resource limits. Docker unavailability causes refusal.

Prooflane also calculates when a review priority would change. One shared contract price affects three invoices together. At eighty two dollars and fifty nine cents, invoice five hundred and one remains first. One cent higher produces a tie; the next cent changes the leader. These are explicit arithmetic assumptions, not AI predictions.

Here is the containment moment. These fixed probes were freshly run from the public site on the existing Vultr virtual machine. Network access failed, a root filesystem write was denied, the inference credential variable was absent, the effective user was non root, and container removal was confirmed. Five observed checks passed.

A reviewer can download the source linked JSON packet and check it again. The original AI packet passed all seven verification groups in the browser and in the offline command line verifier. These checks reconstruct source choices, arithmetic, hashes, and priority boundaries. They do not authenticate the supplier or prove that a supplied contract is true.
